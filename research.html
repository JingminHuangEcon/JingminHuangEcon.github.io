Consumer Search

3 papers
01

Search and Bundling

with Jidong Zhou

How does bundling change consumers’ search incentives and firms’ pricing decisions?

Overview +

We study bundling in multiproduct search markets, comparing separate sales with pure bundling. The analysis examines how restricting consumers’ ability to mix and match products across firms changes the incentive to continue searching, the allocation of demand, and firms’ pricing incentives.

02

Pandora’s Problem with Interdependent Values

with Renjie Bao, Sanxi Li, and Jun Yu

How does learning about correlated alternatives change when to stop searching?

Overview +

We study sequential search when observing one option changes beliefs about the alternatives that remain. Under suitable conditions on positive dependence and learning, optimal search follows an interval rule: both sufficiently low and sufficiently high observations can induce stopping. We characterize this rule, its response to search costs, and how it evolves as beliefs are updated.

03

The Limits of Search Algorithms

with Xiaoyu Chen and Yibo Lian

Which market outcomes can a platform implement by choosing the order in which consumers search?

Overview +

A platform commits to a search algorithm that maps prices to a search order. Sellers then set prices and consumers search sequentially. We characterize the prices that search algorithms can implement and study how seller-optimal, consumer-optimal, and socially optimal algorithms differ.

04

Optimal Hierarchical Incentives and Monitoring Allocation

with Guopeng Li, Zhang Xu, and Wei Zhao

How should incentives be organized when downstream teams observe upstream effort?

Overview +

We study a project carried out by two teams in sequence. Downstream agents observe upstream effort, while the principal observes only project success. The problem is to design success-contingent wages that induce full effort at minimum cost. Monitoring allocations summarize the downstream responses induced by a contract and provide a way to characterize optimal incentives.

05

Who Must Sue, and for What? Claims, Advances, and Smart Contracts

with Sanxi Li, Tong Wang, and Jianye Yan

Claims, advance payments, and the choice between court and smart-contract enforcement.

Overview +

This project studies how legal claims and advance payments shape contractual incentives. It compares traditional court enforcement with automatic smart-contract enforcement, focusing on commitment, flexibility, and the hold-up problem.

06

Selling Training Data

with Wei Zhao and Renjie Zhong

Designing and pricing training datasets for buyers with different private information.

Overview +

A monopolistic seller designs menus of supplemental training data for buyers who possess different private datasets. We study how horizontal and vertical differences in information interact with obedience and incentive constraints, and how these forces shape the structure of the seller’s optimal menu.

1 paper
07

Critical Material Constraints in EV Markets: Implications for Product Line Design and Infrastructure Development

with Luyi Gui and Atalay Atasu

Product line design, material scarcity, and the consumer benefits of charging infrastructure.

Overview +

We examine how critical material constraints shape electric vehicle manufacturers’ product design and pricing. Scarcity creates a trade-off between vehicle quality and sales. The analysis connects the consumer welfare effects of material availability and charging infrastructure to firms’ endogenous product line choices.

08

Public Goods in Directed Network

with Yang Sun, Fanqi Xu, and Wei Zhao

A graph-theoretic approach to the provision of public goods in directed networks.

Overview +

We investigate decentralized public goods provision in directed networks. The analysis relates specialized equilibria to graph-theoretic kernels and uses this connection to study equilibrium existence, uniqueness, network reciprocity, and stability.

1 paper
09

Platform: from Utilizing to Creating Network Effect

with Jinmeng Shi

Competition between platforms when users can become content creators.

Overview +

We develop a model of two-sided platform competition in which users can endogenously become producers. The analysis studies how platforms’ pricing and subsidy decisions influence creator conversion and how equilibrium conversion compares with the social optimum.
